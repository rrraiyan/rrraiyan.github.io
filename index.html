01

Is a new training centre and recording studio worth the money?

I built a five-year valuation for a planned training centre and recording studio in Dhaka. Its 24 linked sheets run from course-by-course revenue, equipment and running costs through tax and cash flow to a combined DCF (discounted cash flow). I built the discount rate up from the Bangladesh 10-year bond yield, Damodaran's country risk data and a start-up premium, and checks inside the model tie out the balance sheets, cash and tax.

On the headline numbers the project clears its hurdle rate. A large share of first-year revenue, though, is the company's own work billed to the centre and studio. Take that out and the plan doesn't pay back within five years, so the decision rests on how much of that work the company would really send their way. Press the button to take it out.

Excel · DCF valuation · CAPM · Scenario analysis

Cumulative cash flow after tax, scaled to the upfront investment. Amounts are left out because the model is confidential.

02

Deep learning won inside the window it was tuned for. Past it, trend fitting won.

I forecast Adobe's share price with two models: N-HiTS, a deep-learning model, and Prophet, which fits a trend and seasonal cycles. I tuned both to forecast 60 days ahead on 2014 to 2018 prices, then forecast 2019 from a single starting point.

Up to 60 days, N-HiTS was more accurate. Beyond that it was forecasting further than it was trained for, and Prophet's trend held up better: at 250 days its error was about half of N-HiTS's. Drag the horizon to see where the lead changes.

Python · Deep learning · Walk-forward validation

An agent that reads tender portals so people don't have to.

Teams that bid for contracts lose hours scanning procurement portals, and most of what they read is irrelevant. I built TenderScreener for BuildNation. It reads about 1,000 new notices a day, keeps about 40, has an AI model score each against the company's services, and emails a short ranked digest.

05

An AI travel agent that plans the whole trip.

Tell TripMate where and when you want to go. It works out what it needs to know, calls live data tools for weather, visa rules, flights, hotels and places, does the budget arithmetic in code, and writes a day-by-day plan from what came back.

For flights and hotels it pulls live prices and gives you a direct link to each one on Skyscanner or Booking.com, ready to book. It also recommends restaurants and sights with real addresses. You can use it in a browser or on Telegram.

Visa answers go through a three-layer check. We added it after an early version told a Bangladeshi traveller they could get a visa on arrival in Thailand, which was wrong.

Node.js · LangChain · React · Built with two classmates

06

Predicting the first-week box office of Avatar: Fire and Ash.

I scraped 5,000+ films, benchmarked 11 models and picked an explainable boosting model to forecast the first-week U.S. gross of Avatar: Fire and Ash. For a 3,800-theatre release, its raw answer was $103M.

I didn't trust that number. Tested on The Way of Water, the same model had come in 2.35× too low, because the franchise outperforms what its budget, cast and history predict. So I scaled the forecast by 75% of that gap (1.76×), holding back a quarter for sequel fatigue. That gave $182M.

The film opened on 3,800 theatres and made $153.8M in its first week. The raw model was 33% under; the corrected forecast was 18% over.

What I'd change: the correction still overshot. I'd test the 75% damping on earlier sequels instead of setting it by judgement, and report a range alongside the single number.

Python · Web scraping · Feature engineering · Explainable AI

07

A YouTube studio I run from one dashboard.

YouTube Autopilot finds trending topics that fit the channel, researches them, writes and fact-checks a script, narrates it, adds footage and captions, and packages one long video with 2 or 3 Shorts. Every claim in the script must quote a source, and the code rejects any quote it can't find in that source.

From the dashboard I approve topics and videos, edit titles and tags, and handle comments. It reads new comments, drafts replies and posts the ones I approve, or posts simple ones by itself within a daily limit when I switch that on. It learns from what I approve and reject. Uploading the video stays my job.

Python · Generative AI · FFmpeg · YouTube API · Cloudflare Workers

  1. Spots a topic that's rising.

  2. Reads source pages on it.

  3. Writes the script, following a playbook for the niche.

  4. Checks the script's claims against the sources.

  5. Narrates it with a speech voice.

  6. Adds stock footage and captions with FFmpeg; cuts 2 or 3 Shorts.

  7. I watch it, approve it, and upload it myself.

What does it mean to be good at using AI at work?

In 2026, Bangladesh Bank and the Cabinet Division both told their staff how not to use AI. No standard says what using it well looks like for people in office jobs. My internship report, written at BuildNation, proposes one: a framework of 28 competencies in 8 domains at 4 levels, for professionals who use AI but don't build it.

I built the draft from 10 international frameworks, tested it against Bangladeshi standards, laws, policies and 18 course outlines, and revised it with a survey of 44 professionals and two interviews with BuildNation's Chief Programs Officer.

Survey of 44 professionals; "without reviewing it" means at least once in the past month. Separately, only 2 of 18 Bangladeshi AI course outlines name a way of checking AI output.
